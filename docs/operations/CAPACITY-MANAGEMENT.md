# Capacity management

The configured filesystem Storage root, Platform data root, package root and (when configured) backup archive root are observed through `ICapacitySourceV1`. A snapshot includes source, UTC timestamp, total, free and computed used bytes, pressure (`Normal`, `Warning`, `Critical`, `Unknown`), completeness and a diagnostic. These are volume observations: roots on one volume can report the same free and total values. The snapshot is best effort: other processes may consume space immediately after observation. The Storage provider checks known-size writes against the available free space and a configurable safety reserve before writing; a later disk-full failure still has to be handled by the normal write path.

Settings under `Platform:Capacity` are `ReserveBytes` (default 1 GiB), `WarningFreeFraction` (default 0.15) and `CriticalFreeFraction` (default 0.05). Fractions measure free bytes divided by total bytes. Critical is reached at or below the reserve or critical fraction; warning is reached at or below the warning fraction. Invalid, missing or contradictory measurements fail closed for preflight. Threshold values are validated at startup. A critical reading never triggers destructive retention.

The administrator can read capacity snapshots with `platform.capacity.read`. The same endpoint is available to Modern and Classic UI. Snapshot values are volume-level, not an accounting of all HISPANIA-owned bytes. The current source reports `OwnedBytes` as unknown; directory walking is intentionally absent from the health path. External Library media remain outside owned storage.

Backup creation estimates the sizes of known SQLite stores and owned Storage/package files before staging, then checks the destination volume against the configured reserve. PostgreSQL dump size remains unknown; this check is only a lower bound. Backup restore performs its existing target-size preflight. Package staging, transcode temporary files and database growth do not yet have a shared capacity preflight. SQLite DELETE can reuse pages without shrinking the file; no automatic VACUUM is performed. PostgreSQL maintenance remains PostgreSQL's concern. Pressure is an operational signal, not a promise that a future write will succeed.
